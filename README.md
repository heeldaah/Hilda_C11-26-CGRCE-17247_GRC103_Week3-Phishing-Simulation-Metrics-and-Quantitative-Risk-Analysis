## Project Overview

This project presents a phishing simulation assessment and quantitative cybersecurity risk analysis conducted for GlobalTech Inc. as part of the ICDFA GRC Engineering Cohort 11 practical laboratory.

The assessment evaluates employee susceptibility to phishing attacks across three simulated campaigns, estimates potential financial exposure, assesses proposed security controls and provides prioritised recommendations to improve the organisation's security posture.

## Objectives

- Analyse phishing simulation results across three campaigns.
- Calculate phishing engagement and credential submission rates.
- Estimate potential financial losses using Single Loss Expectancy (SLE) and Annualised Loss Expectancy (ALE).
- Evaluate the effectiveness and return on investment (ROI) of proposed security controls.
- Apply statistical confidence intervals to interpret campaign results.
- Prioritise cybersecurity risks and recommend appropriate mitigation measures.

## Campaign Summary

The assessment covered 430 simulated phishing emails across three campaigns.

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Emails sent | 430 |
| Emails opened | 354 |
| Emails clicked | 257 |
| Credential submissions | 105 |
| Personal data entries | 38 |
| Overall credential submission rate | 24.42% |

### Key Findings

- Campaign B, involving an IT password reset message, recorded the highest credential submission rate at 30%.
- Campaign A recorded an 18% credential submission rate.
- Campaign C, targeting executives and their assistants, recorded a 15% credential submission rate.
- The overall credential submission rate was 24.42%, indicating a need for stronger phishing awareness and identity security controls.

## Financial Risk Analysis

The financial assessment used the following assumptions:

- Single Loss Expectancy (SLE): $4,350,000.
- Annual Rate of Occurrence (ARO): 1 illustrative occurrence per year.
- Probability of a major breach: 25%.

Based on these assumptions:

**Illustrative Annualised Loss Expectancy (ALE): $1,087,500**

The estimate is illustrative and should not be interpreted as a validated financial forecast. The assumptions require further validation using historical incident data and business-specific risk information.

## Security Controls Evaluated

The following proposed controls were assessed:

1. **Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA):** Evaluated for its potential to reduce the risk of credential compromise.
2. **Security Awareness Training:** Assessed for its potential to reduce phishing susceptibility and employee interaction with malicious emails.
3. **Email Filtering:** Identified as an additional preventive control requiring effectiveness data before its financial benefits can be quantified.

## Key Recommendations

- Prioritise MFA implementation across employee accounts, particularly privileged and high-risk accounts.
- Conduct targeted phishing awareness training, focusing on IT-related password reset requests.
- Strengthen email filtering and phishing detection capabilities.
- Implement additional verification procedures for sensitive requests involving credentials or personal information.
- Conduct periodic phishing simulations to measure improvements in employee awareness.
- Monitor control effectiveness through measurable security metrics.

## Methodology and Limitations

The assessment used the supplied phishing simulation dataset and instructor-provided financial assumptions.

Campaign rates, projected credential submissions, confidence intervals and control ROI were calculated using the available data.

Important limitations include:

- Projected credential submissions do not represent confirmed account compromises.
- The annual loss estimate depends on illustrative assumptions.
- Control effectiveness estimates require validation in a real organisational environment.
- The campaign samples may not represent the entire employee population.

## Conclusion

The assessment highlights employee phishing susceptibility as a significant cybersecurity risk for GlobalTech Inc. Campaign B presents the highest credential submission rate and should receive immediate attention.

A combination of MFA, targeted security awareness training, email security controls and continuous monitoring is recommended to reduce exposure and strengthen the organisation's overall risk posture.
